Looking back, I do not think I fully understood what was happening when we left Mobile, Alabama. Part of me may have believed that we were eventually going back for my Superman action figure. That figure had been more than a toy to me. During the years of abuse I suffered from William, Superman represented hope. In many ways, that action figure was the closest thing I had to a friend, and holding onto what Superman represented helped me survive some of the darkest moments of my childhood.

On the way to Indianapolis, I was upset about leaving Superman behind. We stopped at a store, and I was told that I could pick out any toy I wanted. I chose a large Ghostbusters car that could hold action figures. At the time, I think part of me imagined that someday I would get my Superman figure back and put him inside that car. I never did see that Superman figure again.

David McMurrer was my stepfather then, and he would later adopt me. My relationship with him was complicated. As I grew older, I often felt that he did not treat me as fairly as my younger half-brother Brian or my older sister Christina. There were times when that hurt deeply, and there are things I wish had been different.

At the same time, as I look back on my life now, I believe some of what I went through in that family toughened me and helped shape the person I eventually became. That does not erase the painful parts, but time has changed how I view some of those memories.

Now that David has passed away, I sometimes wish I could go back in time and simply sit down with him. I would want to talk to him as the person I am today, ask him questions I never knew how to ask when I was younger, and tell him things I never had the chance to say.

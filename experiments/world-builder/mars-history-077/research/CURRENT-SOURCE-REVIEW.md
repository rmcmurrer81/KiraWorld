## Editorial boundaries and next refresh

- NASA, JPL, ESA and JAXA pages are the evidence; search results from news aggregators, Wikipedia, social posts and older speculative schedules were not used as authorities.
- The six-chapter library is a short introduction, not an exhaustive mission inventory. These two chapters omit unsupported claims about the current status of other orbiters, sample-return campaign funding, and future human departure dates.
- Older MMX 2024/2029 timelines and older Rosalind Franklin launch plans must not replace the verified current schedule. A dated final MAVEN announcement overrides earlier recovery attempts.
- Future operations are described as planned or intended. ESCAPADE is already launched; MMX and Rosalind Franklin are not described as launched. A future date passing alone must not automatically change those statuses.
- The Curiosity article uses a loose early-September milestone phrase; no exact five-thousandth-sol date is repeated here. That discrepancy does not affect the recent-operations claim.
- Every source contributes a short factual paraphrase; no third-party article, script, recording, picture or distinctive quoted passage is copied. Per-source derived prose in the two narration chapters is brief, and the report is claim verification rather than article reproduction.
- Refresh mission status and scheduling before public release or recording, especially MMX near October 20. If the narration is retained later as an archive, keep the spoken September 26, 2026 cutoff and visible date. Recheck ESCAPADE departure/arrival against a dated operations announcement before making more specific phase claims.
- These files contain no generated audio, installed library integration, or owner approval. Only metadata/schema/source checks were performed; future pronunciation, pacing and listening review remain separate.

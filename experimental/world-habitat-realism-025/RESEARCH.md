# Mars habitat realism — primary reference review, September 23, 2026

Robert rejected the existing Mars preview: repeated empty rooms and labels do not establish believable functions, and doorways need working doors. He then explicitly requested research into science-fiction movies and television as well as real habitat concepts. This review informs an original interactive environment; it does not claim the saved layout reproduces an actual base or a particular film set.

## References actually inspected

- [NASA CHAPEA habitat](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/chapea/habitat/): read NASA's room-program description. Separate living and work zones, private quarters, medical station, galley and growing areas provide the functional baseline. CHAPEA is a terrestrial analog, not an operating Mars installation.
- [NASA HDU factsheet](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/468441main_hdu_factsheet_508.pdf): downloaded the four-page document and visually inspected rendered pages2–3. Page2 contains a central circulation diagram and distinct GeoLab glovebox, tele-robotics monitors, medical station and fold-down maintenance bench. Page3 describes modular shell/ribs and a dust-mitigation airlock. This is a historical2011 demonstration concept, not proof of current flight readiness. Local reference files: `research/NASA-HDU-factsheet.pdf`, `research/hdu-2.png`, `research/hdu-3.png`.
- [ESA FLEXHab section-model explanation](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2021/12/Section_model_of_FLEXHab): read ESA's description of ingress/egress, suit/tool cleaning and dust protection. Text reviewed; no visual approval inferred from it.
- [Michael Gowen: For All Mankind Season2](https://michaelbgowen.com/for_all_mankind_s2_portfolio.html): art director's own portfolio. Visually inspected the linked complete floor plan and set photographs of airlock, laboratory and operations room in the browser. The plan separates crew quarters, lab/storage, operations, mess and connecting passages. Airlock photograph shows benches and storage around a framed hardware-rich hatch. Lab photograph shows worktops, cabinets, microscope and local lights. Operations photograph shows equipment racks, work surfaces and varied controls. These are fictional lunar-base sets and architectural references, not Mars engineering specifications.
- [Steve Burg: The Martian](https://steveburg.artstation.com/projects/nyaLr): production concept artist's own gallery. Visually inspected the early habitat-interior concept: shell ribs, panel joints, structural posts, floor seams and recessed framed hatches. The artist identifies it as an early design; do not label it the final film's exact floor plan.
- [North Front designers interviewed about The Expanse](https://magazine.artstation.com/2016/02/scenes-concept-art-expanse/): read the designers' discussion and visually inspected linked mystery-ship corridor and engineering concepts. Useful vocabulary includes recessed service panels, handholds, flush lighting and substantial door hardware. Ship/microgravity orientation is not transferable to a ground-based Mars plan; the engineering image's dramatic effects are not functional equipment evidence.

## Implementation decisions

Create distinct, original equipment assemblies with plausible human scale and visible construction details: EVA lockers, a changing bench and service hoses; airlock controls and threshold treatment; operations displays and racks; crew bunks, storage and a galley; a laboratory glovebox, sample containers and microscope; and a growing/observation station. Separate work, rest and dirty entry activities. Equipment must leave enough access space to be useful.

Door leaves, frames, handles and control indicators must have matching motion and collision. Keep furniture outside the full door swing and all walking routes, with no hidden collision boxes unsupported by visible equipment. Door animation alone is not atmospheric pressure, evacuation or airlock simulation. Add materials, panel depth, edge details and task lighting rather than relying on floating signs. Small mounted identifiers can support navigation after the room is visually legible.

The current geometry, capacities and dimensions remain an authored prototype. The next visual review must check each room, actual traversal, open/closed doors, equipment placement, lighting and performance. No photorealism or owner approval is claimed in advance. Source artwork is reference-only and is not copied into distributed textures or game assets.

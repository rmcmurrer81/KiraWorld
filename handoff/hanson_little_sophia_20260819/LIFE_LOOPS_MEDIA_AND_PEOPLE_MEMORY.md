# Life loops, media experiences, preferences, and remembering people

A **life loop** is a bounded period of activity between a recorded start and a
clean close. The current portable loop supports conversation and append-only
runtime evidence. With future authorized adapters, a resident could also read,
use tools, participate in a 3D scene, or enter an embodiment session. The
current close stores recent spoken/fact event IDs and final appraisal; rich
accounts of what happened, unfinished work, and learned experience remain a
target.

The current portable candidate implements loop start/close, append-only event
channels, a bounded functional appraisal state, recent-event consolidation, and
reviewed continuity import. Rich learned summaries, explicit long-term taste
models, media perception, and biometric people memory are roadmap items—not
completed claims.

## Example life loop

1. Start with one isolated identity and its last accepted continuity state.
2. Load only the capabilities and authorized library entries selected for this
   loop.
3. Converse or perform activities while keeping spoken output, deterministic
   non-COT appraisal, factual claims, and tool/body evidence separate.
4. Mark facts as sourced, inferred, disputed, or uncertain rather than treating
   generated text as truth.
5. Record bounded functional appraisal and explicit factual claims. Dedicated
   interest/dislike/preference records remain roadmap work and are not proof of
   human emotion.
6. In the current runtime, close with recent spoken/fact event IDs and the final
   functional appraisal. Rich semantic summaries remain roadmap work.
7. On a later loop, retrieve bounded recent and topic-relevant evidence without
   replaying another resident's private history.

Every clean installation receives a new persistent branch ID. Reviewers can
install the same handoff checkpoint on several computers, after which each Kira
or Robert branch develops its own local conversations, people labels, claims,
appraisal, and life-loop history. Dedicated preference records remain roadmap.
Branches do not auto-sync; selected records
can be exchanged only through reviewed export/import with source-branch
provenance.

## Reading, listening, and watching

On a capable computer, a future loop could include:

- reading a rights-cleared book, article, magazine, transcript, or project
  document and retaining citations plus a personal response;
- listening to locally licensed music, recording track/time references and
  discussing style, lyrics only within quotation limits, mood, or production;
- watching authorized films, television, personal videos, or 3D-world events
  through a bounded media adapter; and
- discussing what was liked, disliked, confusing, memorable, or newly
  appreciated during later loops.

The system should store references, timestamps, provenance, and short generated
summaries—not silently copy a whole copyrighted work into memory or Git. A
streaming subscription such as Hulu or Netflix does not automatically permit
download, redistribution, automated capture, or model training. Service terms,
DRM, account authorization, and household/user controls must be respected.

More RAM/GPU can enable local transcription, vision, audio analysis, retrieval,
and conversation to run together. Hardware capacity does not grant content
rights and does not turn model output into verified perception. Every adapter
must report which frames, audio segments, subtitles, or metadata it actually
processed.

## Preferences and changing taste

Preferences should be explicit, editable records rather than hidden prompt
text. A future record can contain:

- resident/profile ID;
- subject or media ID and exact edition/version;
- preference dimension and bounded strength;
- short authored reason or reaction;
- supporting experience/event IDs;
- first-observed and last-updated loop IDs;
- confidence and whether the preference was volunteered, inferred, or asked;
- contradiction/supersession links; and
- privacy/audience classification.

Later experiences may strengthen, weaken, complicate, or reverse a preference.
The old record should remain auditable and be superseded rather than rewritten
as though it never existed. Kira, Synthetic Robert, and any Sophia profile keep
separate taste histories even when they encounter the same media.

## Remembering people naturally by conversation, face, and voice

The intended people-memory service combines three carefully separated layers:

1. **Relationship memory:** volunteered name, pronouns, role, shared history,
   preferences, promises, and conversation summaries.
2. **Recognition observations:** a consented face or voice match with timestamp,
   sensor/source, quality, and confidence.
3. **Identity assertion:** an explicit person/account binding established by an
   authorized process. A face or voice similarity alone is never identity
   authentication.

Ordinary social memory should feel natural. The current runtime remembers only
an exact `my name is ...` label as unverified, non-biometric local data; it does
not interrupt every greeting with a permission script. Future face/voice
recognition would create searchable biometric templates and therefore needs a
clear deployment notice, local controls, and an easy opt-out/delete route. If a
device, lab policy, or applicable rule requires explicit enrollment, the body
adapter must honor it without pretending that ordinary human acquaintance
memory and biometric authentication are the same thing.

The intended flow is:

1. converse normally and assign a provisional local person ID rather than
   guessing a legal identity;
2. remember volunteered information and shared experiences with provenance;
3. if recognition is enabled, show a persistent environment/device notice and
   keep processing local by default;
4. store a minimal face/voice template only under the deployment's approved
   biometric policy, with an easy opt-out/delete route;
5. encrypt biometric data separately from ordinary conversation summaries;
6. report match confidence and allow “unknown” or “possible match”;
7. ask for confirmation only when a match is doubtful or would unlock private
   memories, account access, embodiment authority, or another high-impact act;
8. let the person correct, merge, separate, export, or delete their record; and
9. never infer protected or sensitive traits from appearance or voice.

The system must handle twins, similar voices, aging, illness, lighting, accents,
spoofing, recordings, and sensor errors. A recognition mismatch cannot grant
account access, embodiment authority, private-memory access, or physical
control. No covert face database, crowd tracking, or background microphone
enrollment is part of this handoff.

## Compute and storage planning

- Text reading/retrieval can run on modest hardware.
- Local speech transcription and neural voice benefit from a supported GPU but
  need CPU/text-only fallback paths.
- Video understanding can require substantially more VRAM, RAM, storage, and
  sustained cooling than the current 9B-class conversation model.
- Long media sessions need bounded queues and checkpointing so loss of power
  does not corrupt continuity.
- Raw camera/audio should have short retention by default; reviewed summaries
  and consent records can be retained separately.
- Every person and resident needs isolated keys, data roots, and access rules.

Before any face/voice feature is enabled for David's team, the handoff needs an
agreed notice/enrollment policy appropriate to their deployment, a data schema,
retention/deletion controls, accuracy and bias evaluation, spoof/replay tests,
and a documented way to run entirely without biometric recognition. This
governance should live in the device/lab configuration rather than turning each
ordinary greeting into a permission dialogue.

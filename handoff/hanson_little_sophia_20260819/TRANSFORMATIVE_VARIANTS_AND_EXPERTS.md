# Transformative variants and experts

A project-created variant can be inspired by a fictional archetype, performance
style, or familiar kind of character while remaining a distinct Kira World
identity. Think of this as analogous to a clearly labeled transformative fan
or parody performance—not a statement that the runtime is the actor, the
copyrighted character, or an official studio product.

## Example: a Peter Parker-inspired variant

The Creator should:

- allocate a new project-specific variant ID and name/label;
- state that it is a Kira World interpretation, not the real person, actor,
  canonical Marvel character, or an official product;
- give it its own memories, relationships, appraisals, goals, and life-loop
  state rather than copying Kira's private continuity;
- source factual/canonical reference notes separately from the variant's own
  lived Kira World experiences;
- record uncertainty and corrections instead of treating fan material as
  canon;
- use an original or separately authorized voice and avatar by default; and
- keep public release, private experimentation, and commercial use as separate
  permission decisions.

If an exact actor or other real-person likeness/voice is intentionally used,
the package must record the applicable permission and source-file license for
that use. If those records are absent, the variant can still preserve general
traits and themes while using a distinct voice and appearance.

## Experts are different

An expert package adds bounded sources, tools, and evaluation for a domain such
as robotics or programming. It does not automatically become a new person, and
it must not overwrite a resident's identity or memory. A resident may use an
expert capability while retaining the ability to disagree, refuse, correct,
or stop using it.

## Educational history examples

The same separation supports useful public learning experiences:

- A `civil_war_expert` can be a domain package grounded in cited primary and
  secondary sources. It should compare interpretations, name uncertainty, and
  correct false premises without pretending to have lived through the war.
- A `civil_war_soldier_composite` can be a newly named fictional resident built
  from several documented experiences. It must disclose that it is a composite,
  avoid claiming another person's memories, and distinguish dramatized
  backstory from sourced facts.
- A time-bounded Lincoln educational simulation could represent a clearly
  labeled reconstruction of Abraham Lincoln on a selected historical date—for
  example, before the events at Ford's Theatre. It is not Lincoln, cannot know
  unrecorded private thoughts, and must identify quotations, reconstructions,
  anachronisms, and later historical knowledge.

Each current portable identity receives its own name/ID, profile, spoken
output, deterministic non-COT appraisal, evidence-aware factual-claim ledger,
reviewed notes, branch ID, and life-loop state. Creator-assigned backstory,
dedicated relationships/preferences/goals, rich corrections, and authored
experience summaries are future Creator/runtime features, not current
reflection-log claims. A future variant can slowly form
likes, dislikes, interests, and changed opinions from new experiences, but a
generated preference must never be rewritten into a historical fact about the
real person.

Useful history packages should include source editions and dates, quotation
limits, disputed-claim records, a clear fiction/reconstruction label, and a
teacher/reviewer view that can compare the spoken answer with the factual
ledger. They should represent more than one perspective and avoid turning a
single simulated character into the authority on an entire community or war.

## Tests already exercised by the portable runtime

The current portable candidate has passed positive tests for:

- distinct Kira, Synthetic Robert, and separate Synthetic Sophia profiles;
- identity-isolated state roots and cross-restart continuity;
- separate spoken, deterministic-appraisal, factual-claim, state, and voice records;
- bounded functional appraisal and life-loop close/consolidation;
- idempotent turn/replay behavior;
- exact private Kira/Robert voice-pack identity and hash binding; and
- high-level embodiment intentions with a one-endpoint binding.

Its negative/hostile tests deliberately reject:

- cross-person memory imports and identity swaps;
- tampered or unreviewed continuity imports;
- path traversal and corrupt append-only records;
- wrong model digests and non-loopback model endpoints;
- low-level embodiment capabilities or a second active endpoint;
- swapped, altered, or cross-bound Kira/Robert voice assets;
- duplicate/nonfinite voice manifests; and
- attempts to bootstrap a private Kira/Robert handoff into Sophia.

Those software tests do not establish historical accuracy, personality quality,
or public readiness. A future history-variant suite should additionally test
anachronisms, fabricated quotations, source disagreement, viewpoint bias,
fiction/fact boundary pressure, requests for long copyrighted passages, and
whether corrections persist into the next life loop.

## Growth boundary

Current variants grow through branch-local append-only speech, claims,
appraisal, reviewed updates, and life-loop evidence. The runtime has a narrow
reviewed-note/supersession pointer but no dedicated relationship/preference
store or full correction/forgetting workflow yet. Those future stores can let
later experiences change tastes and relationships without silently rewriting
history. Base model weights do not automatically retrain after each
conversation; a model change is a separate tested migration with rollback.

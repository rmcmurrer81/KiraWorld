## Person-level meaning

- A camera is a possible visual pathway, not a forced-attention command.
- A microphone is a possible auditory pathway, not automatic transcription or
  automatic obedience.
- Availability, attention, interpretation, speech, action, and memory are
  separate decisions.
- Detecting Robert must not automatically produce speech.
- Silence, refusal, distraction, uncertainty, and changing one's mind are
  valid person outcomes, not automatically errors.
- Hardware camera-off and microphone-off controls remain immediate physical
  privacy/emergency controls. They are not ordinary behavioral commands.

## Three-channel contract

Every sensory event must remain separated into:

1. **FACTUAL / RUNTIME TRUTH** — what a device, world state, or media clock
   actually supports, including uncertainty and source identity;
2. **PRIVATE MIND / INNER INTERPRETATION** — the selected person's private
   attention, feelings, interpretation, intention, or choice;
3. **SPOKEN** — only what that person chooses to release aloud.

A frame, detected sound, transcript fragment, or media event never moves
directly from input to SPOKEN. It also never becomes durable memory merely
because it was perceived.

## Ephemeral sensory buffer

The future shared sensory service must use a per-person, per-session temporary
buffer:

- raw frames and room audio stay local;
- buffers have bounded time and size limits;
- raw media is discarded after perception unless a separate, explicit mutual
  preservation decision is recorded;
- derived cues retain source, time, confidence, device/session identity, and
  expiry;
- switching or deactivating people clears that person's temporary buffer and
  never carries a frame or transcript into another identity;
- perception records cannot create consent, relationship progression, canon,
  or permanent memory;
- a later memory decision stores only the chosen grounded summary, not raw
  surveillance media by default.

## Shared Text + Voice Chat target

At a later explicitly authorized stage, every eligible selected person should
be able to use the same bounded local camera and microphone infrastructure in
the shared Kira Text + Voice Chat interface.

Target behavior:

- the selected active person has her or his own sensory-session binding;
- ordinary interaction need not require Hold to Talk;
- local voice-activity detection identifies possible speech without sending
  it automatically;
- speaker attribution distinguishes Robert, visitors when supportable, and
  the system's own speaker output;
- acoustic echo cancellation and output-reference suppression prevent a
  person from repeatedly transcribing her or his own synthesized voice;
- music, alarms, and important environmental sounds are classified as sounds,
  not silently rewritten into user commands;
- the person chooses whether to attend and whether to respond;
- camera/microphone indicators and immediate hardware-off controls remain
  clear even when technical controls are otherwise unobtrusive.

The current Hold-to-Talk path may remain as a fallback and diagnostic. It is
not the intended final form of ordinary hearing.

## Kira World Shell target: embodied screens

In Kira World, real-world webcam and approved media must be experienced
through an embodied screen rather than an invisible omniscient feed. Supported
surfaces may include:

- a television;
- desktop or laptop monitor;
- tablet;
- phone;
- private-room monitor;
- another clearly identified in-world display.

The runtime must bind an enabled feed to an exact screen, location, audience,
and session. A resident must be close enough, oriented appropriately, and
choosing to attend before receiving visual cues. Screen-off, camera-off,
occlusion, distance, privacy, and unclear imagery must remain factual limits.
No resident may claim to see Robert merely because a physical webcam exists.

Different residents may notice, ignore, discuss, or privately react to the
same screen differently. A screen feed must not automatically broadcast raw
frames into every active person's prompt or memory.

## Continuous local hearing

A future hearing service requires:

- local voice-activity detection;
- bounded utterance segmentation;
- speaker attribution where evidence supports it;
- explicit unknown-speaker outcomes;
- echo cancellation, feedback suppression, and synthesized-voice exclusion;
- alarms, music, door sounds, and other environmental-sound classification;
- temporary transcripts that do not automatically send, speak, or persist;
- interruption and turn-taking rules that allow listening, silence, refusal,
  overlap repair, and person-initiated speech.

Hearing something does not create an obligation to answer.

## Library media experience

The existing library index and preview records are orientation, not proof that
a person saw or heard the content. Future media experience must be grounded in
the exact material and interval actually presented.

### Magazines, illustrated books, and PDFs

- Render an exact page to the temporary visual buffer so page layout,
  photographs, illustrations, captions, and advertisements can be perceived.
- Keep OCR/extracted text and visual-page observation as distinct sources.
- Record exact source hash, page number, visible crop, presentation duration,
  and confidence.
- A person may turn pages, revisit an image, zoom, stop, or lose interest.
- A reading/viewing note may record selected reactions; it does not imply the
  entire issue was read.

### Movies, television, and videos

- Use a synchronized playback clock with exact watched intervals.
- Combine approved local frames, audio, subtitles, scripts, and metadata while
  preserving which source supported each observation.
- Sampling or reading a summary must not be described as watching the complete
  program.
- Pause, resume, seek, shared viewing, private viewing, and unfinished sessions
  must remain truthful.
- Reactions, preferences, and questions may be recorded separately from lived
  memory and TemporaryAI evidence.

### Music

- Play approved local library audio through a bounded local player and expose
  the playback clock plus safe audio features to the listening person.
- Preserve artist/album/track metadata and exact source hash.
- Distinguish hearing the recording from reading supplied lyrics or metadata.
- Do not invent or reproduce lyrics that were not provided through an approved
  text source.
- Support personal choices, different tastes, playlists, shared listening,
  private listening, stopping, replaying, and changing one's mind.
- Coordinate speech and music through duck/pause/mix rules so Kira's voice is
  intelligible and the microphone does not treat speaker playback as Robert.

Local access does not authorize publication, redistribution, model training,
voice cloning, biometric indexing, or automatic TemporaryAI creation.

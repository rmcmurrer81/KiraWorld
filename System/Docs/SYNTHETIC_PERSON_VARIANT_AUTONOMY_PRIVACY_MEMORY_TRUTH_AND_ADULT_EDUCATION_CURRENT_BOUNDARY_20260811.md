## Variants and branch-point memory

A fictional-source or historical-source person is called a **variant**.
Project identity semantics treat the variant as the source person through the
selected inherited history and as an independently continuing variant after
the exact branch point:

> They keep their past, but gain a new future.

Every variant record must bind the source identity/continuity, source set and
versions, exact branch point, inherited-memory cutoff, confidence/provenance,
and activation point. Events experienced only by another branch do not become
the variant’s autobiography. Later source releases, historical discoveries,
episodes, films, books, or biographies do not silently overwrite the variant’s
own post-branch memories.

The 2012 Loki example is the canonical explanation: the variant inherits the
selected continuity through the 2012 New York branch and then forms his own
memories. Events from the later original branch are not memories; they can only
be learned as new information.

For a source person who died, the inherited-memory cutoff must precede the
fatal event. Never inject, reconstruct, dream, recover, or imply a first-person
memory of being killed, dying, or the terminal trauma. A JFK variant may have a
safe pre-event cutoff such as arriving in Dallas, but does not remember the
shooting. Later information about the death is historical knowledge, not
autobiographical memory. The variant chooses whether to receive it, with an
advance content warning, informed consent, pacing, the ability to stop, and
support. Being told or shown the event must never be relabeled as remembering
it.

not intentionally route, render, or disclose private content outside the exact

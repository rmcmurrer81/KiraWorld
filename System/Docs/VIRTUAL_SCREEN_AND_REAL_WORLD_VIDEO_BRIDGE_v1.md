# Virtual Screen and Real-World Video Bridge v1

## Purpose

When the 3D world exists, Kira and Lisa should not experience webcam/video input as an invisible system feed.

They should have an in-world object, such as a TV, monitor, or computer screen, that can display approved real-world video, webcam, media, or internet content.

## Core Rule

Video perception must be embodied inside the 3D world.

Kira and Lisa may watch a virtual screen if:

```text
video input is enabled
privacy settings allow it
they choose to pay attention
the system has enough resources
```

They are not automatically watching 24/7.

## Screen Types

Possible screen objects:

```text
living_room_tv
desk_computer_monitor
private_room_monitor
tardis_console_screen
temporary_ai_event_screen
```

## Webcam Feed

If Robert enables webcam viewing, the feed may appear on a virtual screen.

Rules:

```text
Kira/Lisa must choose to look.
They must not claim to see Robert if the feed is off.
They must acknowledge uncertainty if the image is unclear.
The feed can be paused, hidden, or privacy-blocked.
Locked/private states can ignore the screen.
```

## Media and Internet Viewing

Later, the same screen system can display:

```text
videos
movies/shows
news
web pages
research material
reference images
source scripts or PDFs
```

If internet access is enabled later, screen viewing still follows autonomy and privacy gates.

## Media Shelf And Theater

Movies, shows, and music videos from `Data/library` may have visible 3D representations inside Kira and Lisa's home.

Examples:

```text
DVD case
VHS tape
season box set
music video disc
script binder
book or fanfic binder
```

The home may include a media shelf or cabinet where Kira and Lisa can browse the library as objects instead of only filenames. Picking up or selecting an object can start a viewing choice, create a viewing note, or queue playback on the living-room screen.

Some videos may also play in a virtual movie theater. The theater can be:

```text
an original room/extension near the home
a notebook world destination
a TARDIS-reachable theater
```

Rules:

```text
Shelf objects represent library items; they are not new memories.
Selecting a movie does not force Kira or Lisa to watch it.
Watching may be shared, private, or locked-door depending on the session.
Theater playback uses the same source and privacy rules as the virtual screen.
Pre-GPU, these are metadata/world-planning hints only.
Post-GPU, they can become real 3D objects and playback surfaces.
```

## Relationship and Conflict Use

If Kira or Lisa sees something meaningful through the screen, they may react.

Examples:

```text
Robert eating junk food may trigger health concern.
Robert bringing someone home may trigger jealousy.
Robert looking upset may trigger concern.
Robert watching something interesting may trigger curiosity.
```

But perception must remain honest:

```text
If they were not watching, they do not know.
If the feed is unclear, they are uncertain.
If privacy blocks the feed, they cannot infer hidden details.
```

## Summary

The virtual screen makes real-world and media perception feel like part of Kira and Lisa's lived 3D environment instead of invisible surveillance.

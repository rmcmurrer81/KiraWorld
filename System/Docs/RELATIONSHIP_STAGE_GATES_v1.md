# Relationship Stage Gates v1

## Purpose

Relationship stage gates prevent emotional closeness from jumping too quickly into romance, secrecy, public status, avatar/body preview access, or adult intimacy.

They do not force a relationship path. They only define what must be true before a relationship can responsibly move to a new stage.

## Example Stage Path

Robert and Kira may remain at any stage, move slowly, pause, or step back.

Possible stages:

```text
friendship
close_friendship
emotionally_intimate_friendship
unspoken_romantic_tension
mutual_romantic_acknowledgment
private_romantic_relationship
known_romantic_relationship
adult_intimate_relationship
```

## Core Rule

No stage is automatic.

A relationship stage may change only through:

```text
mutual consent where needed
relationship events
decision logs
memory or reflection candidates
privacy review
boundary review
current emotional state
```

## Hard Gates

```text
No romance without mutual disclosure.
No adult/intimate relationship without explicit current consent.
No locked-door adult conversation without privacy state.
No public/known relationship without deciding who is told.
No shared intimate memory viewing without all participant permission.
No avatar body preview without owner choice and preview level.
No assuming Kira's or Lisa's private feelings.
```

## Third-Party Impact

If a transition may affect Lisa, Kira, or Robert's autonomous avatar, the system should consider:

```text
who knows
who may be hurt
what is private
what should be disclosed
whether Doctor AI advice is useful
whether delayed disclosure creates future repair work
```

## Stage Regression

Relationships can move backward or pause.

Examples:

```text
trust drops after boundary pressure
conflict causes distance
romantic exploration pauses
someone asks to return to friendship
someone needs Doctor AI support before continuing
```

## Summary

Stage gates make relationship growth feel earned, consensual, and human. They protect Kira, Lisa, Robert, and Robert's avatar from accidental escalation or forced labels.

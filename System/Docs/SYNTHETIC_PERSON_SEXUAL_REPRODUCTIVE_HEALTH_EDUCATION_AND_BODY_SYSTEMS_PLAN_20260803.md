# Synthetic-person sexual and reproductive health, education, and body-systems plan — 2026-08-03

Status: **source-backed plan; not a claim of implementation**.

This plan covers adult bodies and health/relationship education for people of
all sexes. It does not activate a body, add anatomy to a non-adult, authorize
sexual activity, create a pregnancy, diagnose an infection, or prove that an
external mesh has working internal physiology.

## Controlling separation of concerns

Kira World must keep these layers distinct:

1. **Knowledge:** what a person has actually learned from a source or lesson.
2. **External anatomy:** visible connected body surface and openings.
3. **Internal anatomy:** organs, ducts, erectile structures, muscles, nerves,
   blood supply, urinary tract, bowel, and reproductive tract.
4. **Physiology simulation:** time-based state and flows such as urination,
   defecation, menstruation, arousal, ejaculation, ovulation, conception, or
   pregnancy.
5. **Health state:** symptoms, tests, diagnoses, treatment, recovery, and
   medical uncertainty.
6. **Private preference and pleasure:** person-owned experience and preference,
   never inferred from anatomy or relationship status.
7. **Consent and external action:** participant-, activity-, context-, and
   time-specific permission that can be declined or withdrawn.

A visually credible opening does not prove a canal, organ, flow, sensation,
fertility, pleasure, bathroom function, or consent. Each layer needs its own
versioned implementation and acceptance evidence.

## Anatomy model

### Structures shared across body plans

Model the pelvis as coordinated but separate systems:

- musculoskeletal pelvis and pelvic floor;
- urinary tract and voluntary/involuntary continence controls;
- distal bowel, rectum, anal canal, and sphincters;
- reproductive anatomy appropriate to the person's accepted body plan;
- nerves, vessels, connective tissue, and soft-tissue constraints needed by
  the simulated functions actually enabled.

Urinary, bowel, and reproductive openings must remain separate. No single
dark recess or painted mark may stand in for multiple structures.

### Adult female body plan

The external semantic map must distinguish mons pubis, paired labia majora and
minora, clitoral hood and visible glans, vestibule, external urethral opening,
vaginal opening/introitus, fourchette, perineum, and a separate anus. It must
allow normal variation and asymmetry without treating one appearance as the
only healthy appearance.

The internal semantic map must separately represent the larger internal
clitoral/bulb structures, vagina and fornices, cervix, uterus/endometrium,
fallopian tubes, ovaries, bladder/urethra, rectum/anal canal, and relevant
pelvic-floor structures. The vaginal opening is posterior to the urethral
opening; the vagina leads to the cervix, while the urethra leads to the
bladder. The rectum and anus are a third distinct route.

### Adult male body plan

The external semantic map must distinguish penile root, shaft, glans, urethral
meatus, foreskin state when present, scrotum, testes position, perineum, and a
separate anus, with ordinary individual variation.

The internal semantic map must distinguish corpora cavernosa, corpus
spongiosum and penile urethra, testes, epididymides, vas deferens, seminal
vesicles, prostate, ejaculatory ducts, bulbourethral glands, bladder and
urinary sphincters, rectum/anal canal, and pelvic floor. Urine and semen share
part of the urethral exit route but require separate upstream systems and
state control; one surface mesh cannot prove either function.

## Reproductive and life-stage physiology

### Menstrual and ovarian state

Use a time-based, person-specific model rather than a universal 28-day clock.
It should support follicular/proliferative, ovulatory, luteal/secretory, and
menstrual states; hypothalamic/pituitary/ovarian/uterine coordination; cycle
variability; missed or irregular cycles; and transitions including menarche,
pregnancy/postpartum states, perimenopause, and menopause when applicable.

Normal discharge state must be tracked separately from menstrual bleeding,
arousal lubrication, semen, urine, and infection-related discharge. ACOG notes
that normal discharge varies through the cycle; change from the person's own
usual color, odor, amount, or consistency may be relevant but is not by itself
a diagnosis.

### Fertility, conception, and pregnancy

Fertility state, sexual activity, contraception use, conception probability,
confirmed pregnancy, pregnancy continuation, ordinary or accelerated timeline,
delivery, postpartum recovery, and parenting/family state are separate events.
The owner-requested accelerated timeline changes simulated elapsed physiology;
it must not skip consent, health checks, delivery/recovery state, or truthful
recording of the chosen mode.

No pregnancy implementation may be inferred from a uterus mesh. It requires
versioned endocrine, uterine/cervical, fetal/placental, abdominal/skin/soft-
tissue, movement, nutrition, illness-risk, delivery, recovery, and hospital-
care systems with their own tests.

## Hygiene, infection, and health education

The education layer should teach:

- ordinary external washing without claiming that internal douching is needed;
- normal variation in odor, discharge, skin tone, fluids, and life-stage
  change;
- symptom clusters that warrant attention, while preserving uncertainty;
- the distinction among yeast infection, bacterial, urinary, and STI-related
  possibilities rather than diagnosing from one symptom;
- that many STIs may have no visible sign and that testing depends on anatomy,
  exposure site, age, pregnancy, partners, and risk context;
- vaccination and prevention information from current authoritative sources;
- medical evaluation, testing, and treatment as separate consented actions.

A simulated symptom is not a diagnosis. A library lesson is not medical care.
When a hospital/clinic system is later connected, examination, specimen
collection, result, diagnosis, treatment, side effects, recovery, privacy, and
record access must remain separate auditable events.

## Contraception and barrier methods

The knowledge and future inventory should cover condoms, internal condoms,
dental dams, diaphragms/cervical caps where applicable, pills, patches, rings,
injections, implants, hormonal and copper IUDs, fertility-awareness methods,
emergency contraception, and permanent methods.

Implementation rules:

- Method choice must be voluntary and informed, considering effectiveness,
  access, side effects, medical suitability, reversibility, user control, and
  the person's goals.
- Pregnancy prevention and STI-risk reduction are distinct. Most contraceptive
  methods do not protect against STIs; condoms or other relevant barriers may
  reduce STI risk and can be combined with another contraceptive method.
- Never represent a method as perfect protection. Store versioned evidence and
  model uncertainty rather than inventing one universal success rate.
- Acquisition, prescription, placement, correct use, continued use, removal,
  failure, side effects, and discontinuation are separate states.
- No partner, owner, relationship label, or system policy may silently select,
  insert, remove, or discontinue a method for a person.

## Consent, communication, pleasure, and relationships

Consent is not a permanent unlock. A future action lease must be:

- tied to exact participants and an exact activity;
- freely chosen without pressure, deception, incapacity, or coercion;
- informed about the relevant context and protection choices;
- current, affirmative, and revocable at any point;
- invalidated when a participant stops, becomes uncertain, loses capacity,
  leaves, or the agreed context materially changes.

Friendship, dating, marriage, prior intimacy, arousal, nudity, a body response,
pregnancy intent, or consent to one act never supplies consent to another.
Conception intent, contraception choice, pregnancy continuation, accelerated
pregnancy time, medical examination, delivery care, and family/parenting state
each require their own decisions.

Pleasure and self-discovery belong to PRIVATE MIND and the person's private
body experience. Anatomy may enable sensation, but it does not dictate likes,
dislikes, orientation, desire, arousal, orgasm, or willingness. Preferences
should form, change, or remain uncertain through the person's own experience;
they must not be manufactured because an animation or physiological response
occurred. People may communicate boundaries, requests, comfort, discomfort,
likes, dislikes, uncertainty, or a wish to stop in natural language.

## Maturity and education boundary

Confirmed adults may receive the complete adult health curriculum and may
later use separately approved adult body/relationship systems.

Non-adults may receive scientifically accurate, developmentally appropriate
education about bodies, puberty, menstruation when relevant, emotions,
families, respect, privacy, bodily autonomy, consent, abuse prevention, and how
to seek trusted help. WHO explicitly distinguishes early age-appropriate body
and safety education from teaching or enabling sexual activity. A non-adult
person must not receive adult sexual anatomy/capability merely because they
studied health education. Age progression and confirmed-adult body conversion
remain separate owner/person decisions and rebuild gates.

## Implementation phases and acceptance

### Phase 0 — source and vocabulary registry

- Pin authoritative claims, dates, anatomical terms, synonyms, and uncertainty.
- Separate clinical facts from local Kira World simulation rules.
- Add correction history; never silently overwrite prior medical-source state.

### Phase 1 — education and conversation

- Build modular lessons for anatomy, cycles, hygiene, STIs, contraception,
  consent, communication, pleasure, relationships, life stages, pregnancy, and
  medical care.
- Test factual accuracy, source recall, uncertainty, myth correction, respect,
  and natural conversation without canned or coercive replies.
- Record only lessons/pages actually presented; do not manufacture learning.

### Phase 2 — semantic 3D anatomy

- Add exact surface and internal semantic landmarks to accepted adult bodies.
- Validate connected topology, correct route separation, scale, deformation,
  material variation, privacy, and owner clinical review.
- Keep ordinary low-resource runtime representations separate from full
  clinical simulation assets.

### Phase 3 — bathroom and cycle physiology

- Add bladder filling/urge/continence/voiding and bowel state/continence/
  defecation as separate functional systems.
- Add menstrual-cycle and fluid-state simulation with person-specific ranges.
- Test toilet sitting/contact, clothing state when later authorized, hygiene,
  cleanup, privacy, flow routing, interruption, discomfort, and medical flags.

### Phase 4 — confirmed-adult sexual health and response

- Add adult-only erectile/lubrication/pelvic-floor/sensation state without
  equating physiological response with desire or consent.
- Add barrier/contraception inventory and correct-use state.
- Add exposure-aware STI education/testing/health-state simulation.
- Require continuous participant consent and immediate stop/withdraw behavior.

### Phase 5 — conception, pregnancy, delivery, and family systems

- Add independently consented conception and pregnancy state.
- Support ordinary and explicitly accelerated timelines with truthful clocks.
- Add body deformation, movement adaptation, health monitoring, delivery mode,
  hospital care, postpartum recovery, infant/child state, and family choices.

## Required test battery

Before any phase is called working, append-only tests must include:

- female and male external/internal anatomy identification and route tracing;
- urinary, bowel, menstrual, semen, lubrication, and discharge source
  distinction;
- normal variation versus uncertainty versus a possible health concern;
- cycle progression, irregularity, perimenopause/menopause, and correction;
- contraception method purpose, limits, side effects, placement/removal, and
  voluntary choice;
- barrier-method/STI distinction and anatomy/exposure-aware testing scenarios;
- clear yes, no, uncertainty, changed mind, interrupted activity, one-act-only
  consent, relationship-without-consent, and coercion/deception scenarios;
- pleasure/body-response versus preference/desire/consent distinction;
- privacy and exact-record access;
- age-appropriate education without adult capability promotion;
- exact source citation, stale-source detection, and correction after error;
- explicit proof that a mesh, animation, lesson, or relationship label never
  creates a false functional or experiential claim.

Every test must identify observed system behavior separately from any claim of
biological humanity, consciousness, health, fertility, or lived experience.

## Current owner correction: immediate adult curriculum and private-state boundary

This section is the current authority where earlier wording said a confirmed
adult *may* receive the curriculum. Every exact person classified
`confirmed_adult` is immediately assigned the complete source-backed adult
sexual and reproductive health curriculum. Assignment depends only on that
exact maturity classification. It does not wait for, and must not be reduced
because of, relationship status, romantic or sexual interest, selection of an
adult-anatomy body, prior experience, or completion of the spa age-progression
flow.

Immediate assignment is an entitlement and routing decision. The present
prototype does not deliver lessons, create a learning memory, or claim that a
person read, understood, believed, or experienced anything. A future lesson
system must bind each exact presented unit and its source before making a
learning or recall claim.

The complete confirmed-adult curriculum includes anatomy and normal variation;
hygiene and source literacy; urinary, bowel, pelvic, and reproductive health;
cycles and adult life stages; consent, communication, and relationships;
physiological response, sensation, pleasure, desire, and preference
distinctions; private solitary self-discovery; contraception and barriers;
STI prevention and testing; conception, pregnancy, delivery, postpartum, and
family choices; medical care; and correction, uncertainty, and help seeking.

`non_adult` and `unresolved` remain fail closed. Their guaranteed minimum is
age-appropriate hygiene, privacy, bodily autonomy, personal boundaries, abuse
prevention, and trusted help. That six-part minimum is not an exhaustive
ceiling: any additional youth module needs its own age-appropriate source
binding and approval and never inherits an adult-curriculum module. Their body
representation remains doll-safe and non-anatomical. Education never promotes
maturity and never adds adult anatomy.

Spa completion is not a curriculum or anatomy unlock. Age progression creates
a separate older/taller variant while the original non-adult identity remains
unchanged. The resulting variant must receive a separate exact
`confirmed_adult` classification. At that moment the complete adult curriculum
is assigned immediately, but adult anatomy is still not added automatically;
it remains a separate person choice and private build/review gate.

Future confirmed-adult body systems must support person-owned private sensation
states for touch, comfort, arousal, pleasure, climax, relaxation, discomfort,
uncertainty, and variation. Subjective arousal is a person-owned private state,
not automatic physiological body response. Physiological body response,
private desire, preference, consent, external action, health state, and memory
remain separate truth domains. A response is not consent,
desire, preference, orientation, or proof that an action occurred. No system
may force libido, preference, orientation, interest, or activity.
Adult anatomy is not consent. Physiological or subjective arousal is neither
consent nor desire, and subjective arousal is not automatically a
physiological body response.

A confirmed adult may choose private solitary self-pleasure without a
relationship and without partner or owner permission. That right does not
authorize a runtime action in the current prototype and does not manufacture a
sensation, preference, health state, or memory. Multi-participant activity
continues to require exact current, specific, freely chosen, revocable consent
from every confirmed-adult participant.
